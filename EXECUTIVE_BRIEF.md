# NBA Franchise Investment Advisory
## Executive Brief — Strategy Consulting Report
**Prepared by:** Ajay Ramesh  
**Analysis Period:** 2013–2024  
**Data Coverage:** 30 NBA Franchises | 11 Seasons | 321 Team-Season Records

---

## Objective

NBA franchises invest hundreds of millions of dollars in player payroll each season, yet higher spending does not guarantee better performance. This analysis evaluates which franchises generate the highest return on payroll investment and identifies teams that are efficient, undervalued, or high-risk from a business perspective — providing a data-driven framework for franchise investment decisions.

---

## Methodology

The analysis began with raw game-by-game NBA data covering 33,316 game records from 2013 to 2024. The data was cleaned by removing unnecessary fields, standardizing date formats, and engineering a win/loss indicator variable. Team-level payroll data was integrated to calculate Cost Per Win — the core ROI metric of this analysis.

Teams were classified into four strategic quadrants based on total wins and cost per win efficiency, benchmarked against league medians. A 3-year rolling average was applied to identify performance trends, and each franchise was assigned an investment rating of BUY, HOLD, MONITOR, or AVOID based on performance efficiency and long-term risk trajectory.

---

## Key Findings

**Finding 1 — Payroll Does Not Buy Championships**
The Golden State Warriors appear in both the top 5 most efficient franchises (2014-15, 2015-16) and the top 5 worst investments (2019-20). This reveals that their early dynasty was built on emerging talent at low cost — not high spending. When those players became expensive, efficiency collapsed. The business lesson: invest in emerging players early, before the market prices them in.

**Finding 2 — Small Market Teams Deliver the Best ROI**
The Oklahoma City Thunder achieved the lowest cost per win in 2023-24 at just $1.65M per win. As a small market franchise with limited brand power, they compensate through superior talent identification and development. This is a model worth replicating — and worth investing in before the market catches up.

**Finding 3 — Overspending on Declining Rosters Is the Biggest Risk**
The Washington Wizards spent $94M in 2023-24 and won only 15 games — a cost of $6.29M per win. This is not a payroll problem, it is a strategy problem. Continuing to fund an aging, underperforming roster destroys franchise value. The recommendation is to disperse the current roster, absorb short-term losses, and rebuild around underrated emerging talent.

## Risk Assessment

- **86 High-Risk Investment** team-seasons identified across the analysis period
- Teams spending above the $2.23M median cost per win with below-median wins represent the highest capital destruction risk
- The most dangerous pattern: franchises that overpay aging stars rather than investing in development pipelines

---

## Investment Outlook

The NBA is entering a talent revaluation cycle. Franchises that identify and develop emerging players before market pricing adjusts — as Golden State did in 2013-15 and Oklahoma City is doing now — will generate the strongest returns. The window for acquiring undervalued franchises like Orlando and Indiana is narrowing as their performance becomes more visible to the broader market.

Franchises that fail to restructure bloated rosters risk multi-year value destruction, as evidenced by the Wizards, Pistons, and Raptors in 2023-24.

**The Moneyball principle remains valid: efficiency beats spending. Every time.**
